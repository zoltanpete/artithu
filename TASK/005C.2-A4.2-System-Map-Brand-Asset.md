# Task 005C.2 — Art Direction A4.2: System Map as Brand Asset

## Context

We are continuing the ARTIT website art-direction exploration.

The current strongest direction is **A4.1 — Living System Map**. Owner feedback after visual review:

> “Na igen, ez már húz! :) Ilyesmire gondoltam...”

A generated visual reference then pushed the same idea further. The owner explicitly likes that composition and wants us to **borrow from it quite directly**, not merely treat it as loose inspiration.

This is **not a new art direction** and not a redesign from scratch. Build **A4.2 as a focused evolution of A4.1**, keeping the established ARTIT foundation but moving the Hero much closer to the visual character and composition of the generated reference.

The central idea remains:

> **“Értjük a komplexitást, átlátjuk a káoszt, és letisztult struktúrává formáljuk.”**

The system graph should now become the primary visual expression of that promise and a candidate **ARTIT brand asset**.

---

## 1. Primary goal

Make A4.2 feel fresh, contemporary, precise, premium B2B, technical without looking like a developer-tool landing page, structured without becoming sterile, and visually magnetic without becoming flashy.

The intended synthesis is:

**Swiss / Scandinavian functionalism × modern digital typography × systems engineering × restrained data architecture.**

A4.1 proved the concept. A4.2 should give it **more visual confidence, more integration, more colour hierarchy, and more authored composition**.

The page should communicate at first glance:

> messy operational reality enters → ARTIT understands and connects it → a clear, controlled system emerges.

---

## 2. Important visual reference

Use the generated reference shared by the owner as a **strong compositional reference**.

Do not be afraid to reproduce its successful ideas fairly closely where they fit the existing ARTIT design system.

Specifically, the owner likes:

- the much larger system graph;
- the way the graph occupies and activates a large part of the Hero rather than behaving like an illustration inside a rectangle;
- input nodes arranged vertically / semi-irregularly;
- the visually dominant central `FOLYAMAT` node;
- clean structured output nodes;
- thin neutral paths combined with a few strongly accented paths;
- subtle grid / coordinate construction lines;
- the graph extending beyond an obvious local bounding box;
- the feeling that the diagram belongs to the page itself;
- the restrained blue-violet glow / emphasis around the central transformation point;
- small meaningful status indicators;
- large white space around the composition;
- the overall balance between editorial typography and technical system architecture.

The goal is **not pixel-perfect tracing**, but this time it is absolutely acceptable for the implementation to look recognizably related to the generated reference.

Do not intentionally dilute the reference just to make A4.2 look more different.

---

## 3. Keep from A4.1

Retain unless explicitly changed below:

- existing production copy, navigation labels and CTA wording;
- current typography foundation;
- warm light-neutral canvas;
- sharp geometry / essentially no decorative rounding;
- Ink / Deep Blue-Violet working accent direction;
- restrained mono metadata language;
- current header height and corrected header→Hero spacing;
- no fake application screenshots, analytics or business metrics;
- complexity → structure → clarity narrative;
- A4.1’s small page-scoped JS approach;
- keyboard-accessible real HTML controls;
- reduced-motion support;
- separate intentional mobile composition;
- zero impact on production `/` and previous experiments.

This is a prototype refinement, not a production design-system migration.

---

## 4. Remove the questionable Swiss decoration

Remove the A4.1 Hero device:

`SEC / 01 — POZICIONÁLÁS`

and

`01 ───────────────────────── 02`

It feels like stylistic annotation rather than semantically useful information.

Important distinction:

- **remove the prominent labelled horizontal ruler/device;**
- **keep or introduce extremely subtle construction/grid lines inside the overall Hero composition where they genuinely support the system-map aesthetic.**

Those background guides should feel architectural, almost subliminal — not like content the visitor must interpret.

---

## 5. Hero layout — move closer to the generated reference

The Hero should become a single integrated composition rather than “copy column + graph column”.

The underlying grid may still exist technically, but visually weaken the boundary substantially.

### Left side

Keep the current message:

**A szoftver alkalmazkodjon a vállalkozásához. Ne fordítva.**

Keep the existing eyebrow, lead paragraph and CTA labels. Maintain strong editorial typography. Do not reduce the H1 merely to make room for the graph.

### Right / centre / surrounding field

Make the graph significantly larger and more spatially ambitious.

It should:

- occupy most of the right half;
- move further toward the centre;
- visually interact with the whitespace between graph and copy;
- use paths that travel across a wider field;
- allow selected lines / guides to extend substantially beyond the nodes;
- feel like a system laid over the page rather than an SVG picture placed beside the copy.

A few extremely subtle construction lines may span large portions of the Hero, including behind the graph. They must remain quiet enough that the H1 stays dominant.

The result should feel deliberately composed at 1440 and 1920 widths, not simply “bigger SVG”.

---

## 6. Graph vocabulary

Use concrete business-system vocabulary, exploring something close to:

- `EXCEL`
- `E-MAIL`
- `KÜLÖN RENDSZER`
- `KÉZI ADAT`
- `API`

Optionally add a quiet secondary input:

`…ÉS MINDEN MÁS FORRÁS`

if it improves the composition.

These are examples of messy operational inputs, not product capabilities.

Transformation node:

`FOLYAMAT`

Outputs:

- `JÓVÁHAGYÁS`
- `SZÁMLÁZÁS`

Do not add fake KPIs, percentages, monitoring states, charts or fabricated product data.

---

## 7. Graph visual hierarchy

A4.1 is still too uniformly quiet. A4.2 needs **multiple levels of visual emphasis**.

### Neutral network

Most incoming paths should remain thin, low-contrast, neutral grey and calm. Some may be curved. One irregular/dashed route may represent an exception or awkward manual path.

### Critical paths

One or two paths should receive the blue-violet accent. They should immediately make the graph easier to read without making it colourful everywhere.

The accent path may begin at a selected input and visibly travel through `FOLYAMAT` toward an output.

Use stroke weight + colour + opacity together. Do not rely on colour alone.

### FOLYAMAT

Make `FOLYAMAT` the visual transformation centre.

Borrow fairly directly from the generated reference:

- stronger blue-violet border and/or surface;
- restrained halo/glow;
- concentric technical marker or small target-like device;
- slightly greater scale;
- clear visual gravity.

Keep it premium and restrained. Avoid neon/cyberpunk aesthetics.

### Outputs

`JÓVÁHAGYÁS` and `SZÁMLÁZÁS` should feel aligned, stable, resolved and clean.

Small green status dots are acceptable because they have semantic meaning: resolved/operational output.

**Green must not become the ARTIT brand colour.** The main accent remains blue-violet.

---

## 8. Interaction — retain and improve the Living System Map

Keep the graph interactive. It is now part of the concept and potentially the future ARTIT brand grammar.

Desired behaviour:

- hover / keyboard focus previews a path;
- click / Enter commits selection;
- selected node/path becomes clearly emphasized;
- unrelated paths recede;
- clicking another node changes focus;
- clicking the selected node again may clear selection.

If technically reasonable, add a **very restrained sense of recomposition** when a node is selected.

Do NOT build a physics graph and do not introduce a graph library.

Possible approaches:

- slight translation of nearby nodes;
- alternate path geometry;
- subtle repositioning of the selected relationship;
- central emphasis expanding slightly.

The feeling should be:

> “the system reorganizes around the thing I’m inspecting.”

Not:

> “interactive toy”.

Static composition must remain excellent before interaction.

---

## 9. Fix the known focus-outline bug

There is a known A4.1 visual bug:

On hover/focus, the dashed focus outline for `JÓVÁHAGYÁS` and `SZÁMLÁZÁS` is offset and intrudes into the visible node instead of surrounding it correctly. `EXCEL` behaves correctly.

Fix this in A4.2. Do not hide the focus outline.

The final focus indicator must accurately surround the corresponding visible node, have consistent spacing around all interactive nodes, remain visible, and never overlap node text.

Verify this in a real browser for every interactive node.

---

## 10. Background grid / architectural guides

Introduce a **very subtle architectural coordinate field** around the graph.

Potential ingredients:

- one or two vertical guides;
- one horizontal baseline;
- small intersection ticks;
- sparse crosshair marks;
- partial guide segments;
- tiny mono coordinate labels only where genuinely useful.

These must be dramatically quieter than the graph itself.

Think:

**blueprint construction residue**, not “grid background component”.

Avoid a regular graph-paper pattern across the entire viewport. Avoid gratuitous `SEC / X`, `01 / 02`, X/Y labels.

Less but better.

---

## 11. Problem section

Do not redesign it from scratch. A4.1’s move toward the cleaner A3 editorial treatment was correct.

Bring it into greater visual dialogue with the Hero:

- very light tonal section field;
- subtle vertical continuation / guide alignment from Hero where useful;
- diagnostic entries organized as precise signals;
- blue-violet dots/rules used sparingly;
- strong editorial headline.

Keep approved copy.

Do not turn it back into A4’s fake-dashboard-looking diagnostic panel.

Hero = living system.

Problem = structured diagnosis.

---

## 12. Decision section

Keep it quiet.

The emotional progression remains:

**complexity → diagnosis → clarity**

Do not put another large graph here. It should feel like arriving at calm after understanding the system.

---

## 13. Colour

Continue with the current Ink / Deep Blue-Violet direction, but use it more confidently.

Use accent for:

- primary CTA;
- selected graph path;
- active node;
- `FOLYAMAT` emphasis;
- small structural details.

Do not flood large areas with violet.

Neutral black / warm white / soft grey remain dominant.

A small semantic green may appear only for resolved/active status markers.

---

## 14. Typography

Keep the established typography foundation unless there is a genuine technical reason not to.

Sans: headlines, body, navigation, CTA.

Mono selectively: node labels, tiny system metadata, technical annotations, diagnostic indexes.

Do not let monospace dominate. Premium B2B editorial first, technical second.

---

## 15. Motion

Motion must remain restrained and purposeful.

Allowed:

- subtle selected-path transition;
- tiny status pulse;
- very slight graph recomposition;
- central-node emphasis transition.

Not allowed:

- continuous floating nodes;
- decorative particles;
- gratuitous parallax;
- perpetual animated background;
- dramatic spring physics.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` and verify it programmatically. The page must look complete with motion disabled.

---

## 16. Mobile

Do not simply scale the desktop graph.

Create a deliberately composed mobile version.

- H1 remains dominant;
- graph comes after core copy/CTA;
- reduce visible inputs if necessary;
- preserve input → process → output story;
- preserve at least one meaningful interactive path;
- avoid tiny labels;
- avoid horizontal overflow;
- simplify construction guides substantially if needed.

It should feel like a designed ARTIT system-map vignette, not a miniature desktop diagram.

---

## 17. Accessibility

Retain or improve A4.1:

- real HTML buttons for interactive nodes;
- meaningful `aria-label`;
- correct `aria-pressed` or equivalent state;
- keyboard reachability;
- Enter/Space activation as appropriate;
- visible, correctly positioned `:focus-visible`;
- no colour-only state communication;
- meaningful graph accessible description;
- reduced motion fully respected;
- normal heading hierarchy.

Test keyboard behaviour in a real browser.

---

## 18. Technical constraints

- New experimental route only; do not modify previous prototypes.
- No graph/chart library.
- No new dependency unless absolutely unavoidable.
- Keep JS small and page-scoped.
- No external font request.
- No fabricated application UI.
- No production `/` changes.
- Do not prematurely extract a shared Graph component.
- Reuse existing design tokens wherever possible.

---

## 19. Visual-review requirement

Do not declare completion from code inspection.

Render and inspect at:

- 390
- 768
- 1440
- 1920

At 1440 and 1920 explicitly ask:

1. Does the graph feel like part of the page rather than a diagram beside the copy?
2. Is it substantially more visually magnetic than A4.1?
3. Does `FOLYAMAT` clearly read as the transformation centre?
4. Do neutral vs. critical paths create useful hierarchy?
5. Is blue-violet confident without becoming loud?
6. Do construction lines add precision without clutter?
7. Does the H1 still win initial attention?
8. Does the composition resemble the successful generated reference enough to preserve what the owner liked?
9. Does the page communicate “we turn operational complexity into a precise system” without explanation?
10. Could this graph grammar plausibly become recognizable as ARTIT over repeated use?

If #2, #8, #9 or #10 is weak, do another visual refinement pass before completion.
